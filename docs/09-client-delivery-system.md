# Client Delivery System

**STATUS: FRAMEWORK — NOT YET ACTIVATED**

- **Document:** Client Delivery System
- **Stage:** Stage 4 — Delivery and Client Experience
- **Owner:** MPG Founder
- **Last Updated:** 2026-08-28
- **Public Safe:** Yes
- **Authority:** Framework only; no delivery process or service level is approved here

## Purpose

Define the evidence, controls, responsibilities, and client experience required to deliver an authorized service consistently from discovery through closure. The framework is designed to make scope, quality, approvals, changes, handover, and support explicit before routine client delivery.

## Questions This Stage Must Answer

### Discovery through kickoff

- What sales-discovery information must be verified again at delivery handoff?
- What onboarding information, access, materials, approvals, payment evidence, and client responsibilities are required?
- How will requirements be elicited, prioritized, documented, validated, and traced?
- What creates the scope baseline, and which assumptions, exclusions, dependencies, deliverables, milestones, and acceptance criteria form part of it?
- What must the kickoff confirm about roles, communication, schedule, risk, access, decision authority, and escalation?

### Work and communication

- What milestone model fits the service, and what evidence allows work to move between milestones?
- Which communication channel, cadence, response expectations, decision log, and status format are sustainable?
- How are reviews requested, consolidated, time-bounded, approved, and recorded?
- What separates an included revision, defect correction, clarification, and chargeable change request?
- How will scope, timeline, cost, risk, and acceptance be re-baselined after an approved change?
- What happens when the client, MPG, a vendor, or a partner blocks progress?

### Quality, release, and closure

- Which QA checks and acceptance criteria apply before client review and before deployment or release?
- Who may authorize deployment, and what rollback, backup, access, security, and third-party checks are required?
- What handover package, ownership transfer, credentials process, documentation, and training are needed?
- What support, maintenance, warranty, incident, and out-of-scope boundaries apply after handover?
- When is a project accepted and closed, and what financial, access, asset, record-retention, and lessons-learned steps remain?
- How will MPG ask separately for testimonial or case-study permission without treating project acceptance as publicity consent?

## Required Inputs

- An authorized service definition, delivery mode, scope boundary, pricing method, and accepted sale or pilot authorization.
- Qualified discovery, approved proposal and quote, contract or terms evidence, and required payment evidence.
- Capability, capacity, tool, asset, partner, risk, and information-handling reviews.
- Service-specific SOP, QA checklist, acceptance criteria, support boundary, and change-request rule.
- Verified client decision-makers, responsibilities, dependencies, and access requirements in approved private storage.

## Required Outputs

- An end-to-end delivery map with stage entry, exit, owner function, controls, and evidence.
- Sales-to-delivery handoff, onboarding, requirements, scope-baseline, kickoff, status, review, and change-control procedures.
- Service-specific milestone, QA, acceptance, deployment, rollback, and escalation checklists.
- Handover, training where required, support, maintenance, closure, and access-revocation procedures.
- A responsibility model based on functions, not invented employees.
- Permission records for any testimonial, customer name, project result, image, or case study.

## Activation Gate

This framework may be activated only after Stage 3 is accepted and the MPG Founder explicitly authorizes Stage 4. Routine client delivery requires a service-specific process, QA criteria, capacity, commercial terms, and risk controls. A framework or template alone is not evidence that MPG can deliver the service.

## Dependencies

- Sales system and approved commercial model.
- Service lifecycle, launch gates, quality standard, and risk management.
- Capability, tool, asset, service, and partner registries.
- Information-handling rules and approved private client storage.
- Project kickoff, handover, SOP, case-study, and related operational templates.

## What Must Not Yet Be Assumed

- No timeline, milestone count, revision allowance, communication cadence, acceptance rule, warranty, support period, maintenance offer, hosting role, or training commitment is approved.
- No client access, content, data, domain, hosting, software license, or decision-maker is available until verified.
- No deployment is automatically authorized by completion of build work.
- No testimonial, logo use, case study, result claim, or client name is public without specific permission and verified evidence.
- No partner role or service level exists unless documented and approved.
