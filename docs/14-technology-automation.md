# Technology and Automation

- **Document:** Technology and Automation
- **Stage:** Stage 8 — Internal Operations and Automation
- **Owner:** MPG Founder
- **Last Updated:** 2026-08-28
- **Public Safe:** Yes

## Purpose

Define safeguards for evaluating and implementing internal or client-facing technology, workflow automation, integrations, and AI-assisted processes. Automation should solve a clear, stable business problem with accountable human oversight; it must not be adopted for novelty or marketed through vague transformation claims.

## Questions This Stage Must Answer

### Problem and process fit

- What defined business problem, user, trigger, input, decision, output, and desired improvement does the automation address?
- Is the current process understood, sufficiently stable, and worth automating?
- Which steps require human judgment, approval, exception handling, or legal responsibility?
- What happens when data is missing, a rule is ambiguous, a vendor is unavailable, or the automation produces an unsafe result?
- Would a simpler process change or existing tool solve the problem with less risk and maintenance?

### Data, security, and ownership

- What data enters, leaves, persists, or trains a system, and how is it classified?
- What consent, access, segregation, retention, deletion, audit, and geographic requirements apply?
- Who owns client accounts, credentials, prompts, code, configurations, data, outputs, logs, and generated assets?
- Which vendors, subprocessors, models, APIs, licenses, terms, and rate limits are involved, and how current is that review?
- How are secrets stored outside this public repository and access revoked at handover or incident?

### Reliability and operations

- What success, failure, duplicate, timeout, degraded, and recovery states exist?
- What validation, test data, approval checkpoints, human fallback, retry, rollback, and manual runbook are required?
- What monitoring, logging, alert, incident, escalation, correction, and change-control process is proportionate?
- Who maintains the automation, updates dependencies, reviews model or vendor changes, and supports users?
- What availability, accuracy, response, maintenance, and support expectations can be stated realistically?

### Economics and claims

- What build, subscription, usage, integration, monitoring, support, vendor, and exit costs exist?
- How will capacity, vendor lock-in, portability, and a fallback provider or manual path be assessed?
- Which measured baseline and pilot evidence could support a benefit claim?
- How will MPG describe limitations and avoid promises of autonomous, error-free, universal, or guaranteed outcomes?
- If automation is client-facing, which service lifecycle, capability, proof, commercial, and marketing gates apply separately?

## Required Inputs

- A documented current-state process and evidence of a material problem.
- Capability, tool, service, asset, risk, and data-classification records.
- Verified vendor documentation, terms, pricing, permissions, integration limits, and ownership model.
- Defined users, decision authority, exception paths, service boundaries, and maintenance capacity.
- Security, privacy, legal or compliance review where relevant.
- Baseline observations and a controlled test plan.

## Required Outputs

- An automation assessment with problem statement, alternatives, benefit hypothesis, exclusions, and owner decision.
- Current and proposed process maps plus a data-flow and access model.
- A vendor and total-cost assessment with portability and exit considerations.
- Risk, security, privacy, human-oversight, failure-mode, and fallback records.
- A controlled pilot plan with acceptance criteria and realistic claims.
- Test evidence, monitoring specification, runbook, incident process, maintenance owner, handover, and retirement plan.

## Activation Gate

This framework may be activated for internal operations only after Stage 7 is accepted, the MPG Founder authorizes Stage 8, and the underlying process, data controls, ownership, fallback, and maintenance capacity are ready. A client-facing automation requires its own full service-readiness review, validated capability and proof, appropriate pilot, DELIVERY_READY state, explicit market approval, and ACTIVE status before public sale.

## Dependencies

- Tool-adoption, information-handling, risk, quality, public-claims, and WIP policies.
- Capability, service, tool, asset, and partner registries.
- Internal operations, SOP, delivery, support, and incident processes.
- Qualified security, privacy, legal, or regulatory input where required.

## What Must Not Yet Be Assumed

- No process needs automation merely because it is manual.
- No AI, model, platform, integration, account, license, budget, data permission, capability, vendor reliability, or savings is approved or available.
- No tool purchase proves MPG can deliver an automation service.
- No generated output is presumed accurate, safe, unbiased, confidential, or suitable without appropriate validation and human control.
- No vague AI transformation claim is permitted.
- Internal success does not automatically prove readiness for client delivery.
