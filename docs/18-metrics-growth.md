# Metrics and Growth

**STATUS: FRAMEWORK — NOT YET ACTIVATED**

- **Document:** Metrics and Growth
- **Stage:** Stage 9 — Launch, Measurement and Growth
- **Owner:** MPG Founder
- **Last Updated:** 2026-08-28
- **Public Safe:** Yes
- **Authority:** Framework only; no baseline, historical result, target, or forecast is approved here

## Purpose

Define a metrics catalogue that can later help MPG make evidence-based decisions about demand, economics, delivery quality, customer experience, capacity, resilience, and growth. Metrics must have clear definitions and reliable data before targets are considered.

## Questions This Stage Must Answer

### Measurement governance

- What decision will each metric support, and what harmful behavior could it encourage?
- What event, population, period, unit, currency, source, owner, formula, inclusion, and exclusion define it?
- Is the source complete and stable enough to calculate the measure consistently?
- Which measure is operational, financial, customer-reported, attributed, estimated, confidential, or public-safe?
- What baseline period is representative, and what limitation or context must accompany comparisons?
- Which qualified review is required for accounting, tax, privacy, employment, or other regulated interpretations?
- When could an owner-approved target be responsible, and how will it be changed or retired?

### Candidate catalogue

| Candidate Metric | Question It Could Answer | Definition Required Before Use |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified leads | Is suitable demand entering the sales system? | Current qualification rule, unique lead, source, and period |
| Lead-to-client conversion | How often do qualified opportunities become accepted clients? | Numerator event, denominator cohort, period, and exclusions |
| Average project value | What is the typical contracted or recognized project value? | Value event, currency treatment, taxes, pass-through costs, and project set |
| Gross margin | Does delivery economics support the service? | Revenue basis and approved direct-cost allocation |
| Recurring revenue | How much qualifying revenue is contractually recurring? | Eligible contract types, recognition basis, churn, currency, and period |
| Delivery cycle | How long does an eligible project take through defined delivery states? | Start, pause, restart, end, and excluded delay rules |
| Rework | How much effort follows preventable defects or missed requirements? | Rework category distinct from approved client changes |
| Support burden | What post-delivery demand does a service create? | Eligible time, tickets, cost, period, and support boundary |
| Customer satisfaction | How do clients report their experience at a defined point? | Instrument, question, timing, response handling, and sample context |
| Referrals | How much suitable demand comes from attributable referrals? | Referral source, qualified event, duplicates, and permission |
| Capacity and utilization | Can MPG fulfill demand without quality or wellbeing risk? | Available capacity, delivery categories, non-billable work, and safe limits |
| Cash reserve or runway | What resilience does available cash provide? | Private cash source, committed obligations, burn method, and scenario assumptions |
| Service profitability | Which service contributes after its defined costs and support load? | Revenue and cost allocation by service and time period |
| Content-to-lead attribution | Which content can be credibly connected to suitable enquiries? | Identity, touchpoint, attribution model, consent, and confidence limits |

### Growth decisions

- Which evidence would justify improving the current offer, adding capacity, raising quality controls, changing price, pausing demand, or considering the next service family?
- How will MPG separate growth in attention from growth in qualified demand, cash, margin, capability, and delivery reliability?
- What hiring or partner evidence reflects consistent demand, a repeated bottleneck, financial capacity, process maturity, quality risk, and opportunity cost?
- How will founder dependency, client concentration, vendor dependency, and support obligations be visible?
- Which metrics should never be published because they are private, misleading, insufficiently verified, or commercially sensitive?

## Required Inputs

- Stable definitions from the sales, pricing, delivery, support, finance, content, capacity, and service systems.
- Approved private systems capable of retaining source records safely.
- Data-quality, access, retention, privacy, accounting, and review requirements.
- A current service, customer, project, and period taxonomy.
- Owner-defined decisions or hypotheses that measurement is intended to support.

## Required Outputs

- A metric dictionary with ID, purpose, formula, source, owner function, cadence, classification, limitations, and change history.
- Data-quality tests and a correction process.
- Evidence-supported baselines clearly separated from targets and forecasts.
- A private operational review and public-reporting classification.
- Owner-approved target records only when sufficient baseline and capacity evidence exists.
- A compact decision dashboard specification and a review process that records actions, not only numbers.

## Activation Gate

This framework may be activated only after Stage 8 is accepted, the MPG Founder explicitly authorizes Stage 9, and the necessary source processes, private systems, definitions, and controls are reliable. A metric may be reported only after its definition and source pass review. A target or forecast requires a separate owner decision and must not be inferred from the candidate catalogue.

## Dependencies

- Sales, pricing, delivery, content, internal operations, and company-growth frameworks.
- Service lifecycle, quality, risk, information-handling, and public-claims policies.
- Approved accounting definitions and private source systems.
- Service and capability registries plus current owner decisions.

## What Must Not Yet Be Assumed

- MPG has no historical result, baseline, target, benchmark, forecast, revenue, margin, conversion, customer, capacity, satisfaction, or growth rate recorded by this framework.
- Candidate metrics are not mandatory merely because they are listed.
- External benchmarks are not directly comparable until scope, market, maturity, and method are verified.
- Correlation or last-touch attribution does not prove causation.
- A target does not override quality, capacity, cash, privacy, or WIP safeguards.
- Confidential financial, client, workforce, vendor, or performance data must not be stored in this public repository.
