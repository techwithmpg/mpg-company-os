# SOP Library

**STATUS: FRAMEWORK — NOT YET ACTIVATED**

- **Document:** SOP Library
- **Stage:** Stage 4 onward, as each delivery or operations process is authorized
- **Owner:** MPG Founder
- **Last Updated:** 2026-08-28
- **Public Safe:** Yes
- **Authority:** Framework only; no SOP is approved or operational merely by being listed

## Purpose

Define how MPG will create, approve, use, verify, revise, and retire standard operating procedures for repeatable, risk-relevant work. The library should make delivery more consistent without freezing an untested process or publishing confidential operational detail.

## Questions This Stage Must Answer

### SOP selection and scope

- Which repeatable process needs an SOP because inconsistency would create material quality, client, financial, information, legal, partner, or reputation risk?
- Is the underlying process sufficiently defined and tested to standardize?
- What event starts the procedure, what outcome ends it, and what is explicitly outside its scope?
- Which service, lifecycle state, function, system, jurisdiction, partner, or risk context limits its use?
- Should the procedure be public-safe, internal, confidential, or split into public governance and private operational instructions?

### Procedure content

- What prerequisites, inputs, access, tools, assets, roles, approvals, and safety checks are required?
- What ordered actions, decision points, evidence, quality checks, handoffs, and completion criteria must be explicit?
- Which exceptions require stop-work, rollback, escalation, professional review, client approval, or incident handling?
- What records demonstrate completion without exposing credentials or confidential data?
- Which linked templates, checklists, policies, contracts, vendor instructions, or authoritative sources must be current?

### Ownership and lifecycle

- Which function owns the process, approves the SOP, performs it, verifies it, and may authorize an exception?
- What identifier, title, version, status, effective date, review trigger, and change history are required?
- How will a draft be tested under controlled conditions before acceptance?
- What training, acknowledgement, competency evidence, or supervised use is needed?
- What event triggers review, correction, suspension, replacement, or retirement?
- How will obsolete copies and links be withdrawn while decision history is preserved?

## Required Inputs

- An owner-authorized, observed process with defined scope and responsible functions.
- Applicable service, delivery, quality, risk, information, tool, partner, and legal requirements.
- Evidence from actual tests, pilots, incidents, rework, or process reviews.
- Approved SOP template, naming and versioning conventions, and storage classification.
- The capabilities, tools, access, assets, and private systems needed to perform the procedure.

## Required Outputs

- A governed SOP index showing identifier, title, process owner function, classification, status, version, dependencies, and review trigger.
- Approved SOPs with prerequisites, ordered steps, decision points, controls, evidence, exceptions, escalation, and completion criteria.
- A draft, test, acceptance, publication, training, change, suspension, and retirement workflow.
- A service-to-SOP coverage view that identifies missing required procedures before activation.
- Verification records showing whether procedures remain usable and are followed.

## Activation Gate

The library framework may be activated only when the MPG Founder opens the relevant delivery or operations stage. An individual SOP may become effective only after its underlying process is authorized, its information classification and dependencies are ready, it has been tested, and the appropriate owner records acceptance. A required SOP must exist and be usable before the related service passes its delivery-process gate.

## Dependencies

- SOP template, decision register, service lifecycle, launch gates, and service catalogue.
- Delivery system, internal operations, quality, risk, information-handling, partner, and tool-adoption policies.
- Capability, tool, asset, service, and partner registries.
- Approved private storage for confidential procedures and evidence.

## What Must Not Yet Be Assumed

- No SOP owner, reviewer, effective date, review interval, system, or procedure is approved.
- A template, checklist, draft, or generated document is not an accepted SOP.
- Documentation does not prove a capability is validated or a procedure is followed.
- A process should not be standardized before material uncertainty and failure modes are understood.
- Public-safe SOP governance does not authorize publishing credentials, client records, partner details, contract terms, security configuration, or sensitive commercial methods.
- One SOP is not automatically suitable across different services, jurisdictions, tools, or delivery modes.
