## Architecture at a glance

**MPG** is the master company brand. Its current working architecture organizes long-term capability into three pillars:

| Brand domain | Pillar | Strategic capability areas | Current claim boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| MPG Technologies | **BUILD** | Professional business websites, e-commerce, custom software, business systems, AI and workflow automation, integrations, and technical maintenance/support | Strategic capability domain; only registry-approved services may be marketed |
| MPG Media | **GROW** | Digital marketing, social media management, content strategy, paid advertising, SEO/search visibility, analytics, brand support, and conversion optimization | Strategic capability domain; individual media capabilities require separate readiness |
| MPG Trade & Distribution | **CONNECT** | Supplier sourcing, procurement, manufacturer representation, import/export coordination, distribution, wholesale sourcing, and freight/logistics coordination through qualified partners | Strategic/planned domain; partner, legal, jurisdiction, and delivery-role evidence is required |

The words **BUILD**, **GROW**, and **CONNECT** organize the company's direction. They do not prove that MPG currently employs separate teams, operates separate legal entities, owns specialist infrastructure, or sells every listed capability.

## Working brand idea

`Build. Grow. Connect.` is the current working brand idea. It expresses the architecture but is **not** a frozen final tagline. Stage 1 brand work may test its clarity, distinctiveness, audience fit, proof, and usability. Any decision to retain, revise, or freeze it requires explicit owner acceptance.

Until then:

- it may be described as a working idea or organizing architecture;
- it must not be described as permanent, registered, tested, or final;
- no visual identity, slogan ownership, or legal protection is assumed.

## Master brand and domains

MPG is the durable company-level identity. The domain names describe strategic ownership within the architecture:

- MPG Technologies owns the BUILD pillar.
- MPG Media owns the GROW pillar.
- MPG Trade & Distribution owns the CONNECT pillar.

“Owns” here means architectural responsibility. It does not assert incorporation, staffing, licensing, commercial activity, or separate operational capacity.

Service names and public pages must remain subordinate to the authoritative service registry. The existence of a domain must never be used as a shortcut around service readiness.

## Founder/company distinction

### Malcom Gwanmesia — founder brand

Appropriate subjects include:

- leadership and founder direction;
- trust and relationship building;
- documented expertise and project experience;
- learning and capability development;
- accurate lessons, thinking, and industry observations.

### MPG — company brand

Appropriate subjects include:

- approved offers and commercial scope;
- delivery systems and quality standards;
- company processes and client experience;
- evidence-backed outcomes and organizational operations.

The founder may learn broadly without being presented as a “developer + marketer + social media manager + sourcing agent + automation expert + shipping expert + designer.” The company may productize only what it can deliver reliably.

## Public communication boundary

Brand communications must distinguish:

1. **Active offer content** — may solicit clients only for services that are `ACTIVE` and have owner marketing approval.
2. **Capability journey content** — may transparently discuss research, learning, experiments, events, and building without implying an available service.
3. **Thought leadership** — may share accurate lessons, observations, and project experience without turning every topic into a commercial claim.

Example of acceptable future-oriented language:

> We are studying supplier-verification workflows for future MPG Trade operations.

Unacceptable without an approved active record:

> MPG provides end-to-end supplier verification.

## Domain safeguards

### BUILD

Professional Business Websites are the first productization priority. Custom systems and automation follow in the accepted order. AI and automation claims must be tied to defined workflows, data/security review, monitoring, human fallback, error handling, costs, access ownership, and maintenance expectations.

### GROW

Creating posts does not establish full-service marketing capability. Strategy, creative, copywriting, organic social, paid media, tracking, analytics, SEO, and conversion optimization must be assessed separately and may activate separately.

### CONNECT

Trade & Distribution remains strategic/planned until prerequisites are validated. MPG must not call itself a freight forwarder, customs broker, licensed importer, manufacturer representative, distributor, or shipping company unless that description is factually and legally supported. Partner coordination must be described accurately, and partner infrastructure must never be represented as MPG-owned.

## Architecture-to-service rule

The brand hierarchy is not the service catalogue. The flow is:

`Strategic domain → capability assessment → service definition → readiness evidence → lifecycle progression → owner marketing approval → ACTIVE public offer`

The future website may explain the company narrative and capability direction, but active offer pages must be based exclusively on market-approved service records.

## What this document does not decide

This accepted architecture does not decide:

- final positioning, promise, values, tone of voice, tagline, visual identity, or naming system;
- target customer segments, markets, channels, or campaign strategy;
- active service scope, pricing, availability, proof, capacity, or service levels;
- legal structure, staffing, locations, partners, or infrastructure.

Those matters remain governed by their future-stage frameworks and explicit owner decisions.
