# Trade and Distribution

**STATUS: FRAMEWORK — NOT YET ACTIVATED**

- **Document:** Trade and Distribution
- **Stage:** Future capability readiness; sixth in the accepted commercial sequence
- **Owner:** MPG Founder
- **Last Updated:** 2026-08-28
- **Public Safe:** Yes
- **Authority:** Framework only; MPG Trade & Distribution remains strategic and planned

## Purpose

Define the questions and safeguards that must be resolved before MPG undertakes any sourcing, procurement, representation, import or export coordination, distribution, wholesale sourcing, or logistics-coordination activity. The framework is intentionally cautious because delivery is jurisdiction-specific, transaction-risk-heavy, and likely partner-dependent.

## Questions This Stage Must Answer

### Role, scope, and capability

- What precise role would MPG perform in each transaction: adviser, sourcing coordinator, disclosed agent, reseller, distributor, representative, or another legally verified role?
- Would delivery be owned, partner-delivered, or hybrid, and what must be disclosed to buyer and supplier?
- Which sourcing, supplier-verification, procurement, negotiation, documentation, import or export, product, market, and dispute-handling capabilities are validated?
- Which product categories, jurisdictions, customer types, transaction sizes, and activities are in scope or prohibited?
- What evidence demonstrates demand and MPG's ability to produce a defined, repeatable outcome?

### Legal, regulatory, and transaction safeguards

- Which company registrations, licenses, permits, customs, tax, product-standard, labeling, sanctions, consumer, agency, distribution, and local-business requirements apply in each jurisdiction and role?
- Which qualified professional must verify those requirements before a pilot or transaction?
- Who is importer or exporter of record, contracting principal, title holder, risk bearer, and party responsible for declarations, duties, inspections, and product compliance?
- What contract, representation agreement, commission disclosure, insurance, warranty, indemnity, and limitation terms are required?
- How will currency, deposit, escrow or payment method, fraud, non-delivery, quality, chargeback, refund, and counterparty risk be controlled?

### Supplier, partner, and logistics readiness

- What proportionate supplier identity, ownership, authority, capability, sample, quality, reference, facility, documentation, and ongoing-monitoring checks are required?
- Which verification steps can MPG perform competently and which require an independent qualified party?
- Which freight providers, customs brokers, inspection providers, warehouses, insurers, payment providers, or professionals are required?
- Has each required partner passed capability, reliability, role, price, communication, geography, compliance, contingency, agreement, and disclosure review?
- What happens if a supplier, shipment, document, customs process, inspection, payment, or partner fails?

### Economics and client experience

- How are fees, commissions, partner costs, product costs, freight, insurance, duties, taxes, storage, inspection, currency exposure, and contingencies calculated and disclosed?
- Which quotations are estimates and which may be binding?
- What approvals, evidence, communications, tracking, document retention, handover, acceptance, escalation, and dispute processes are needed?
- What capacity and capital exposure can MPG safely support?

## Required Inputs

- Explicit owner authorization to research the trade capability within the WIP policy and commercial sequence.
- Jurisdiction-, role-, product-, and transaction-specific qualified legal, tax, customs, insurance, and compliance advice.
- Assessed capability evidence and public-safe service, tool, asset, and partner records.
- Verified counterparty and partner due diligence stored in appropriate private systems.
- Defined delivery mode, transaction workflow, economics, capacity, risk tolerance, customer need evidence, and pilot boundaries.
- Contracts, disclosures, insurance evidence, fallback routes, and dispute procedures appropriate to the proposed role.

## Required Outputs

- A narrow role and service definition with explicit inclusions, exclusions, jurisdictions, products, delivery mode, and disclosures.
- A capability-gap and prerequisite plan.
- Jurisdiction-specific professional-review records and compliance checklists.
- Supplier, counterparty, partner, transaction, payment, product, logistics, and documentation due-diligence procedures.
- A transaction map with responsibilities, approvals, title and risk transfer, evidence, exceptions, and dispute paths.
- Verified economics, capacity, insurance, contract, incident, contingency, and controlled-pilot records.
- Truthful public language that accurately describes MPG's role and any partner dependence.

## Activation Gate

Research may begin only when explicitly selected as the permitted secondary capability-development track or later primary track. Commercial activation requires its proper place in the accepted sequence, owner authorization, all applicable service readiness gates, validated role-specific capability, qualified professional verification, approved partners and agreements, controlled pilot evidence, DELIVERY_READY status, explicit marketing approval, and ACTIVE status.

## Dependencies

- WIP policy, commercial sequence, service lifecycle, launch gates, partner governance, and public-claims policy.
- Capability, service, tool, asset, and partner registries.
- Risk, quality, information-handling, pricing, sales, delivery, and SOP systems.
- Qualified external legal, customs, tax, insurance, product, and jurisdiction expertise as applicable.

## What Must Not Yet Be Assumed

- MPG is not to be described as a freight forwarder, customs broker, licensed importer, manufacturer representative, distributor, shipping company, or other regulated or contractual role without verified legal and operational basis.
- No supplier, manufacturer, buyer, warehouse, broker, carrier, insurer, adviser, route, country, product, license, capital, insurance, or transaction is approved or available.
- A contact or preliminary conversation is not an official partnership.
- Supplier documents, directory listings, samples, or online reviews are not sufficient verification by themselves.
- MPG does not own partner infrastructure and must not imply that it does.
- Research, travel, tool access, or partner discovery does not authorize a trade offer.
