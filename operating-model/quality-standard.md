## Quality philosophy

> MPG should prefer doing fewer things extremely well over offering many things poorly.

A service does not launch because it sounds profitable. It launches when MPG can reliably deliver a defined outcome within a defined scope, to written acceptance criteria, with adequate capability, controls, economics, capacity, evidence, and owner approval.

## Scope

This standard applies to internal demonstrations, pilots, client deliverables, partner-delivered work accepted by MPG, service processes, offers, and handover/support. It is the baseline; each service must add checks appropriate to its technology, risk, industry, delivery mode, and promise.

## Universal quality requirements

Every delivery must have:

- a traceable service and scope version;
- defined customer outcome, inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, and dependencies;
- agreed deliverables and objective acceptance criteria;
- appropriately validated direct or partner-dependent capabilities;
- controlled inputs, tools, assets, access, and information handling;
- an accountable delivery owner and reviewer;
- planned checkpoints, client approvals, and change control;
- service-specific QA checks performed before release;
- recorded defects, exceptions, remedies, and acceptance;
- secure handover, ownership/access clarity, and support boundaries;
- retained public-safe evidence, with sensitive records in approved private storage.

Unknown acceptance criteria, unbounded scope, or unavailable critical resources block delivery commitment.

## Definition of ready

Work is ready to start only when:

1. qualification confirms service fit and capacity;
2. scope, responsibilities, dependencies, and commercial authority are clear;
3. required contract/terms and payment preconditions are satisfied;
4. required client materials, access, approvals, and data basis are available or scheduled;
5. risks and delivery mode are disclosed and controlled;
6. the delivery plan, milestones, communication route, and acceptance criteria are agreed;
7. unresolved items have owners and do not invalidate the commitment.

Starting early does not convert missing inputs into client-approved assumptions.

## Quality planning and control

The service owner must define a QA checklist that states:

- requirement or risk being checked;
- test/review method and environment;
- expected result or tolerance;
- responsible performer and reviewer;
- evidence produced;
- defect severity and release consequence;
- retest method after correction.

Use independent review where consequences, complexity, conflicts of interest, or professional requirements justify it. Partner output must meet the same client acceptance criteria MPG promises; partner approval does not replace deliverable inspection.

## Change and configuration control

The approved scope, requirements, designs, content, code/configuration, assets, and client decisions must have identifiable versions where changes could cause confusion. A change record must state request, reason, impact on price/time/risk, decision, implementation, and validation. Unapproved requests are not silently absorbed into scope.

## Defects and nonconformities

Classify defects qualitatively according to the service context:

- **Critical:** unsafe, unlawful, security-sensitive, data-loss, unusable core outcome, or serious client/reputation exposure; release stops.
- **Major:** material requirement or core quality criterion is not met; release normally stops until corrected or formally re-scoped.
- **Minor:** limited issue that does not defeat the outcome; correct before release where practical or disclose and agree a dated remedy.
- **Observation:** improvement opportunity with no present nonconformity.

No known defect may be concealed. An exception needs documented impact, risk owner, client/owner authority as appropriate, remedy or acceptance, and review date. Legal, security, privacy, or safety obligations cannot be waived by informal acceptance.

## Definition of done

Delivery is done only when:

- agreed deliverables are complete and traceable to scope;
- all required QA checks have acceptable results;
- critical and major defects are resolved;
- approved minor exceptions are documented;
- client approvals and acceptance are recorded;
- production/deployment and rollback or recovery needs are addressed;
- access, credentials, files, licenses, and ownership are handed over securely;
- training/documentation and support terms are complete where required;
- open actions, warranty/support route, and closure are explicit;
- testimonial or case-study use has separate, informed permission.

Payment, a deadline, or client silence does not prove quality acceptance unless the agreed terms explicitly and lawfully define that mechanism.

## Lifecycle integration

- G5 requires a controlled delivery process.
- G6 is `SATISFIED` only when the service-specific QA and acceptance system is usable and evidenced.
- G8 requires approvals, handover, support, and client communication.
- G10 requires capacity that does not force quality shortcuts.
- G11 requires honest, permissioned proof.

Quality failures may regress a capability, move a gate to `BLOCKED`, or pause a service. An `ACTIVE` service must be paused when MPG cannot maintain its approved standard.

## Improvement and review

After pilots, material defects, complaints, incidents, repeated rework, scope disputes, partner failures, and service changes, review root cause and update the relevant SOP, checklist, training, gate evidence, or scope. Track trends only from real records; do not invent performance baselines or targets.

Professional Business Websites must receive a service-specific QA standard before market approval, including accepted checks for requirements, responsive presentation, browser/device scope, content, forms, links, accessibility, performance, security basics, domain/hosting responsibilities, SEO scope, deployment, backup/recovery, and handover. The exact standard remains a later readiness decision.
