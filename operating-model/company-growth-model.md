## Purpose

MPG will grow when evidence shows that additional operating capacity or specialization is needed—not when a calendar date, job title, or desired appearance suggests it. This model separates company functions from named people and separates organizational maturity from the ten Company OS development stages.

The repository contains no authorized employee roster. One person may own several functions, and one function may later be distributed across employees, contractors, or partners. No role definition is evidence that a person has been hired.

## Organizational modes

### Mode A — Founder-Led

The founder performs most decision-making and delivery. Controls must protect founder capacity, preserve decisions, standardize the first service, and prevent promises beyond available time or capability.

Evidence to remain healthy in Mode A includes bounded WIP, visible workload, repeatable core procedures, documented quality checks, and explicit use of specialists when a capability is not directly validated.

### Mode B — Assisted Delivery

Trusted contractors or partners perform defined, repeatable work while MPG retains clear accountability and interfaces.

Entry evidence should include recurring work suitable for delegation, documented SOP and acceptance criteria, affordable unit economics, an accountable internal owner, partner/contractor due diligence, secure access and information controls, and a fallback. Occasional help alone does not justify representing a team.

### Mode C — Core Team

Recurring demand and financial capacity justify dedicated roles for critical functions.

Entry evidence should include sustained workload, a persistent bottleneck or risk, repeatable processes, reliable cash capacity beyond a short-term project, management and onboarding capacity, measurable role outcomes, employment/contract compliance review, and a viable plan if demand changes.

### Mode D — Divisional Growth

One or more MPG divisions have sufficient demand, economics, process maturity, governance, and leadership to receive dedicated ownership and resources.

Entry evidence should include independently visible service economics and capacity, several validated active offers or a defensible divisional operating case, reliable leadership, cross-division controls, risk and capital oversight, and no dependence on invented scale. Build/Grow/Connect is a strategic architecture, not proof that three operating divisions currently exist at this maturity.

## Mode transitions

Transitions have no arbitrary dates. A proposal must document:

- current mode and the operational constraint being solved;
- demand and workload evidence;
- delivery, quality, client, founder-dependency, and opportunity-cost impact;
- process readiness and role definition;
- full cost, cash resilience, and downside case;
- information access, tool, legal/compliance, and management requirements;
- hire, contract, or partner alternatives considered;
- success measures, review trigger, and reversal/exit plan;
- owner decision.

Growth may be uneven: a service can use assisted delivery while the company remains predominantly founder-led. Record actual arrangements rather than promoting the whole company to create a stronger narrative.

## Functions are not people

MPG must define responsibility for these functions as they become necessary:

- Founder / Strategy
- Sales / Commercial
- Client Delivery
- Technology
- Marketing
- Operations
- Finance / Administration
- Partnerships / Trade
- Customer Success

Every material process or decision should name an accountable **function** and, in the private operating system where appropriate, the assigned person. Public documents must not invent staff or expose private personnel data.

Before adding a person, define:

- outcome and decisions the role owns;
- recurring work and capacity required;
- required capabilities and evidence;
- interfaces, escalation, and quality checks;
- access and information classification;
- measurable indicators without fabricated targets;
- onboarding, supervision, continuity, and exit controls.

## Evidence-based resourcing triggers

Hiring, contracting, or partnering may be justified by:

- consistent qualified demand that current capacity cannot serve safely;
- a recurring delivery bottleneck or support burden;
- financial capacity to sustain the commitment;
- a repeatable process that can be transferred and quality-controlled;
- specialist capability, independence, geography, or license requirements;
- unacceptable quality, continuity, or founder-dependency risk;
- opportunity cost supported by real workload and economics.

It is not justified solely by title prestige, competitor headcount, a temporary spike, unused software seats, or a desire to appear established.

## Relationship to Company OS stages

Organizational modes describe **how MPG is staffed and operated**. Company OS stages describe **which company system is being governed**:

| Stage | Company system | Transition rule |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 0 | Governance and Source of Truth | Current stage; owner acceptance required before Stage 1. |
| Stage 1 | Company and Brand Foundation | Must be explicitly activated. |
| Stage 2 | Services, Offers and Pricing | Must not invent active offers or prices. |
| Stage 3 | Sales System | Requires qualified, capability-aware intake design. |
| Stage 4 | Delivery and Client Experience | Requires controlled processes and quality evidence. |
| Stage 5 | Visual Identity and Design System | Requires accepted brand inputs. |
| Stage 6 | Marketing and Content Engine | Must enforce publication and claims policy. |
| Stage 7 | MPG Website | Active offers derive only from approved service records. |
| Stage 8 | Internal Operations and Automation | Tools follow validated needs and data controls. |
| Stage 9 | Launch, Measurement and Growth | Growth follows evidence, capacity, and economics. |

A company may mature organizationally without skipping a Company OS stage, and a completed document does not authorize a stage transition.
