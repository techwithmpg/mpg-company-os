## Purpose

Risk management supports honest decisions under uncertainty. It does not create false precision or certify that an activity is safe. MPG records qualitative probability and impact unless credible evidence supports a more specific method.

## Required risk categories

Every material service, stage transition, tool, partner, project, and operating change should consider:

- strategic focus and overextension;
- capability and skills;
- delivery, quality, scope, and capacity;
- financial, cash-flow, pricing, currency, fraud, and margin;
- legal, regulatory, contractual, tax, insurance, and compliance;
- partner, supplier, subcontractor, and logistics dependency;
- client concentration and demand dependency;
- founder dependency and continuity;
- privacy, information security, credentials, and data loss;
- brand, claims, client trust, and reputation;
- tool, platform, integration, AI/vendor, and lock-in dependency;
- health, safety, goods, or physical operations where relevant.

The category list is a prompt, not a substitute for context-specific identification.

## Risk record

Each material risk must record:

- stable risk ID, date raised, context, and affected service/project/decision;
- cause, uncertain event, and consequence in a clear “because–may–resulting in” statement;
- category and accountable risk owner;
- existing controls and evidence they operate;
- qualitative probability and impact before further treatment;
- affected clients, partners, obligations, data, or dependencies;
- chosen response, actions, action owners, and review triggers;
- residual probability and impact after treatment;
- acceptance/escalation authority and decision date;
- status and closure evidence.

Use public-safe summaries here. Confidential vulnerability, client, contract, financial, legal, or partner details belong in approved private storage.

## Qualitative assessment

### Probability

- **UNLIKELY:** credible conditions make occurrence exceptional, though still possible.
- **POSSIBLE:** occurrence is plausible under current conditions or evidence is materially uncertain.
- **LIKELY:** current conditions, history, or active warning signs make occurrence reasonably expected.

### Impact

- **LOW:** limited, reversible effect handled within normal work.
- **MODERATE:** meaningful rework, delay, cost, or stakeholder effect requiring management attention.
- **HIGH:** material client, financial, legal, operational, information, or reputation harm; service or project objectives are threatened.
- **SEVERE:** potentially unlawful, unsafe, existential, irrecoverable, or broad harm demanding immediate escalation.

Ratings require written rationale. Do not multiply invented numbers. Uncertainty should normally prevent the rating from being reduced.

## Treatment choices

- **AVOID:** do not start, narrow the scope, or stop the risky activity.
- **REDUCE:** lower probability or impact through preventive, detective, or recovery controls.
- **TRANSFER/SHARE:** allocate defined responsibility through insurance or contract while recognizing MPG retains its own obligations.
- **ACCEPT:** knowingly retain the residual risk with authority, monitoring, and contingency.
- **ESCALATE:** seek owner, qualified professional, client, partner, or regulatory direction before proceeding.

Acceptance is not neglect. It needs a named owner, rationale, time/review trigger, and authority proportionate to the consequence. No internal acceptance overrides law, contract, informed client consent, or third-party rights.

## Escalation and stop conditions

Immediately escalate and normally stop new commitment or release when:

- legal authority, licensing, safety, privacy, security, sanctions, or professional advice is unresolved and material;
- a severe-impact risk lacks a credible control or contingency;
- critical capability, partner, tool, asset, finance, or capacity is unavailable;
- data, funds, goods, credentials, or client operations may be materially harmed;
- a public claim is likely false or permissionless;
- repeated defects or incidents show the approved service standard is not reliable;
- the risk owner cannot monitor or treat the risk.

Protect people and existing clients first, preserve evidence, limit further exposure, and communicate through the appropriate private channel.

## Integration with lifecycle and gates

- `IDEA` and `RESEARCH` records capture uncertainty without making delivery claims.
- Pilot approval requires bounded exposure, success/failure criteria, consent, remedy, and fallback.
- G9 cannot be `SATISFIED` while material legal/compliance risks or required verification remain unresolved.
- G10 must reflect aggregate delivery and founder-capacity risk.
- G12 must address partner interruption, substitution, dispute, and compliance allocation.
- G13 must address claims and reputation risk.
- A new or worsened material risk may move a gate to `BLOCKED`, regress lifecycle status, or require `PAUSED`.

An owner decision to market cannot erase a required legal, quality, or capability condition. Risk acceptance and market approval are separate records.
